This page describes how the app behaves. The privacy policy inside the app is
the authoritative document.

## On your device

Your food diary lives in a database on the device, and photos of meals are kept
in the app's own storage. Your preferences — language, units, appearance,
notification settings — are stored locally too.

## What leaves the device

**Meal photos** are sent to the Kalky backend for analysis, which passes them to
a vision model. This is the whole mechanism by which photographing a plate
produces a nutrition estimate: the picture has to leave the device for it to
work.

**Barcodes and search terms** are sent to the backend to look up products, and
may reach Open Food Facts when a product is not in the Kalky database.

**Your identity** comes from signing in with Google or Apple through Firebase
Authentication. Requests to the backend carry a token that identifies your
account.

## What you control

Delete any diary entry, individually or several at once, and it goes along with
its photo. Meal reminders stay off until you turn them on. Signing out
disconnects the app from your account.

## Self-hosted builds

Kalky is open source, and the public backend has been decommissioned. If you
build it yourself, you run your own backend and connect your own Firebase
project and OpenAI key — so photos and lookups go to infrastructure you control,
and this page describes your deployment, not anyone else's.
