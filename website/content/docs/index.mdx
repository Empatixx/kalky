---
title: What is Kalky
description: A food and nutrition tracker for Android and iOS that reads your meals from a photo.
---

Kalky keeps a diary of what you eat. The point of it is that you should not have
to type much: photograph a plate and it estimates the nutrition, or scan a
barcode and the packaged product is looked up for you. What you get back is a
running total of calories and macronutrients for the day, and a picture of how
that changes over weeks.

The app is in Czech by default and can be switched to English. It runs on
Android and iOS from a single shared codebase.

## What it does

**Reads a meal from a photo.** Point the camera at your plate. The image is sent
to a vision model, which comes back with an estimate of calories, protein,
carbohydrates and fat, plus a guess at what the food actually was. You can
correct any of it afterwards.

**Looks up packaged food.** Scan the barcode and the product is fetched from the
product database, falling back to Open Food Facts when it is not there.

**Remembers what you eat often.** Foods you have logged before are one tap away
under recently used, and you can save anything as your own custom food.

**Adds the day up.** The home screen shows what you have eaten today, how it
compares to your calorie target, and how many days in a row you have logged
something.

**Shows the trend.** Averages for intake and each macronutrient over a period
you choose, along with your weight over time.

## What it is not

Kalky does not give medical or dietary advice. The nutrition it reports for a
photographed meal is an estimate produced by a model looking at a picture — it
is useful for spotting patterns over weeks, not for gram-accurate accounting.
Barcode-scanned products come from a database and are as accurate as the entry
behind them.
